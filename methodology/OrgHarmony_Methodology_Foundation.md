## DISC: Behavioral Style

### What It Measures

DISC measures observable behavioral tendencies across four dimensions:

| Dimension | Measures | High Expression | Low Expression |
|-----------|----------|-----------------|----------------|
| **D (Dominance)** | Approach to problems and challenges | Direct, decisive, competitive | Deliberate, collaborative, cautious |
| **I (Influence)** | Approach to people and social situations | Outgoing, enthusiastic, optimistic | Reserved, reflective, task-focused |
| **S (Steadiness)** | Approach to pace and consistency | Patient, reliable, team-oriented | Fast-paced, flexible, change-seeking |
| **C (Compliance)** | Approach to rules and procedures | Precise, analytical, quality-focused | Independent, flexible, big-picture |

### Scientific Lineage

**Origin:** William Moulton Marston, *Emotions of Normal People* (1928)

Marston was a psychologist who developed the theory that human emotions and behaviors could be understood along two axes:
- Active vs. Passive response to environment
- Favorable vs. Unfavorable perception of environment

This creates four quadrants that map to D, I, S, and C.

**Validation:** Modern DISC implementations (TTI Success Insights, Wiley DiSC, etc.) have been validated through:
- Factor analysis confirming four-factor structure
- Test-retest reliability studies (r = 0.80-0.90 for Natural style)
- Convergent validity with Big Five personality dimensions
- Discriminant validity showing DISC measures behavior, not cognition or values

**Key Research:**
- Marston, W.M. (1928). *Emotions of Normal People*. Kegan Paul, Trench, Trubner & Co.
- Bonnstetter, B.J. et al. (2006). *The Universal Language DISC Reference Manual*. Target Training International.
- TTI Success Insights (2019). *Technical Manual: DISC Assessment Validation Studies*.

### Natural vs. Adaptive: The Critical Distinction

DISC measures TWO behavioral profiles for each person:

| Profile | What It Measures | Stability |
|---------|------------------|-----------|
| **Natural** | Behavioral preference when not under environmental pressure — the "real you" | Highly stable (r = 0.85-0.90 over time) |
| **Adaptive** | Behavioral response to current environment — the "mask you wear" | Variable based on environment |

**Why This Matters:**

The gap between Natural and Adaptive is the **Preference Tax** — the daily energy cost of adapting your natural style to environmental demands.

This is not speculation. It is direct measurement. The assessment captures both profiles, and the gap is arithmetic.

### Threshold Validation

**Why 40 points = High, 20 points = Moderate:**

Research shows a 10-point change in a DISC dimension is the minimum observable difference — the point at which others notice a behavioral shift.

| Gap Size | Meaning | Implication |
|----------|---------|-------------|
| 0-19 | Below observable threshold | Natural alignment |
| 20-39 | One person must shift noticeably | Requires awareness |
| 40-79 | Both must shift significantly | Requires deliberate tools |
| 80-139 | Extreme gap | Requires intervention |
| 140+ | Maximum gap | Unsustainable without structural support |

These thresholds are derived from Standard Error of Measurement (SEM) principles applied to DISC norming samples.

## Values: Motivational Drivers

### What It Measures

Values assessment measures what drives a person — what they find meaningful and worth pursuing.

| Dimension | Drives | Expression |
|-----------|--------|------------|
| **Aesthetic** | Beauty, form, harmony, creative expression | Seeks elegance, artistry, balance |
| **Economic** | Practical ROI, utility, efficiency | Seeks return on investment, pragmatic outcomes |
| **Individualistic** | Uniqueness, independence, freedom of personal expression | Seeks to stand apart, work autonomously, be the distinct voice |
| **Political** | Power, influence, control, leadership | Seeks to lead, direct outcomes, compete to win |
| **Altruistic** | Service to others, humanitarian concern | Seeks to help, contribute, serve |
| **Regulatory** | Order, structure, tradition | Seeks established systems, rules, consistency |
| **Theoretical** | Knowledge, truth, learning | Seeks understanding, discovery, expertise |

### Scientific Lineage

**Origin:** Eduard Spranger, *Types of Men* (1928)

Spranger, a German philosopher and psychologist, proposed that human personality could be understood through the values people prioritize. He identified six "ideal types" based on dominant value orientation.

**Validation:** Gordon Allport and Philip Vernon operationalized Spranger's theory into the *Study of Values* (1931), creating the first validated values assessment. Modern implementations (TTI, Hogan, etc.) build on this foundation.

**Key Research:**
- Spranger, E. (1928). *Types of Men*. Max Niemeyer Verlag.
- Allport, G.W. & Vernon, P.E. (1931). *A Study of Values*. Houghton Mifflin.
- Allport, G.W., Vernon, P.E., & Lindzey, G. (1960). *Study of Values Manual* (3rd ed.).
- Bonnstetter, B.J. (2005). *Personal Interests, Attitudes and Values Technical Report*. TTI Success Insights.

### The Signal Distinction

At the **individual level**, Values assessment creates a SIGNAL, not a confirmed verdict:

- **What is confirmed:** The person's top motivators and their relative strength
- **What is a signal:** Whether the current environment is feeding or starving those motivators

The assessment measures what drives you. It cannot measure what your environment provides. That requires the person's own experience to confirm.

At the **dyadic level**, the gap between two people's Values profiles IS calculable — it's arithmetic on two measured profiles.

## Attributes: Thinking Style

### What It Measures

The Attribute Index measures how a person processes information and makes decisions.

**Two Constructs (Never Merge):**

| Construct | Dimensions | Measures |
|-----------|------------|----------|
| **External Attributes** | Empathy (Heart), Practical Thinking (Hand), Systems Judgment (Head) | How you process THE WORLD |
| **Internal Attributes** | Self-Esteem, Role Awareness, Self-Direction | How you process YOURSELF |

### Scientific Lineage

**Origin:** Robert S. Hartman, *The Structure of Value* (1967)

Hartman developed "formal axiology" — a mathematical approach to understanding how humans assign value. He identified three dimensions of valuation:
- **Intrinsic** (valuing things as unique wholes) → maps to Empathy
- **Extrinsic** (valuing things for their practical use) → maps to Practical Thinking
- **Systemic** (valuing things as instances of categories) → maps to Systems Judgment

**Validation:** The Hartman Value Profile has been validated across multiple studies showing:
- Three-factor structure confirmed
- Predictive validity for job performance in roles requiring specific thinking styles
- Discriminant validity from personality and intelligence measures

**Key Research:**
- Hartman, R.S. (1967). *The Structure of Value: Foundations of Scientific Axiology*. Southern Illinois University Press.
- Pomeroy, L. (2005). *The New Science of Axiological Psychology*. Rodopi.
- TTI Success Insights (2018). *Attribute Index Technical Manual*.

### The Two Variables

Attributes assessment captures TWO pieces of information per dimension:

**Variable 1: Score (0-10)**
- Measures clarity and development of that cognitive capacity
- Higher score = more developed capacity

**Variable 2: Bias (+, -, =)**
- (+) Overvalue: Person REQUIRES this capacity, leans on it heavily
- (-) Undervalue: Person UNDERUTILIZES this capacity, may be frustrated by it
- (=) Balanced: Appropriate relationship to this capacity

**Why Both Variables Matter:**

Two people can have identical SCORES but opposite BIASES:
- Person A: Practical Thinking 7.2, bias (+) — REQUIRES results
- Person B: Practical Thinking 7.0, bias (-) — FRUSTRATED by results pressure

Score gap: 0.2 (looks aligned)
Bias comparison: CONFLICT (fundamental friction)

**The score measures what you HAVE. The bias measures how you USE it.**

### Internal vs. External: Why They're Separate

Internal Attributes (Self-Esteem, Role Awareness, Self-Direction) are **self-concept measures** — how you process your own identity, worth, and direction.

External Attributes (Empathy, Practical Thinking, Systems Judgment) are **world-processing measures** — how you engage with problems, people, and systems outside yourself.

These have different factor structures per I/O Psychology. A person can have strong External processing and weak Internal processing (or vice versa). Merging them collapses distinct psychological dimensions.

## LEVEL 1: INDIVIDUAL

### Question
What does this one person need from their environment to thrive?

### Products
- **Environment Report** — 12-page individual analysis
- **Profile Viewer** — Interactive assessment data display
- **Assessment Debrief** — Guided interpretation session

### Constructs

#### Preference Tax (CONFIRMED)

The daily energy cost of adapting your natural behavioral style to environmental demands.

**Calculation:**
```
PreferenceTax = |D_natural - D_adaptive| + |I_natural - I_adaptive| +
                |S_natural - S_adaptive| + |C_natural - C_adaptive|
```

**Severity Tiers:**
| Points | Level | Meaning |
|--------|-------|---------|
| 0-39 | Low | Minimal adaptation required |
| 40-79 | Moderate | Noticeable daily cost |
| 80-139 | High | Significant energy spent "wearing a mask" |
| 140+ | Significant | Unsustainable long-term |

**Research Basis:**
- Person-Environment Fit theory (Edwards, 1991; Kristof-Brown, 2005)
- Demands-Abilities Fit specifically maps to behavioral adaptation cost
- Meta-analysis shows P-E misfit predicts job dissatisfaction (r = -0.44) and turnover intention (r = 0.29) (Kristof-Brown et al., 2005)

#### Passion Profile (SIGNAL)

What motivates the person and whether the environment honors those drivers.

**What is confirmed:** Top motivators and their relative strength
**What is a signal:** Whether environment feeds or starves them

The assessment cannot measure environment behavior. It measures the person. Whether the environment provides what they need requires their own confirmation.

#### Process Profile (SIGNAL)

Cognitive capacity and processing style.

**What is confirmed:** Scores and biases across External and Internal dimensions
**What is a signal:** Whether environment utilizes or wastes that capacity

Same principle as Passion — the assessment measures the person, not the environment.

### Critical Rule

Level 1 does NOT calculate friction. Friction requires more than one person.

Language from Level 1 (especially "Passion is a signal") must NOT be applied to Level 2 calculations. They are different constructs answering different questions.

## LEVEL 2: DYADIC

### Question
What is the measurable gap between these two people?

### Products
- **Friction Map** — Visual representation of interpersonal gaps
- **Bridge Wizard** — Guided intervention tool
- **Connection Agreement** — Documented behavioral commitments

### Constructs

#### Preference Friction (CONFIRMED)

The behavioral style gap between two people using Natural DISC scores.

**Calculation:**
```
PreferenceFriction = |A.D - B.D| + |A.I - B.I| + |A.S - B.S| + |A.C - B.C|
```

**Always use Natural scores.** Friction lives in the gap between underlying preferences, not current coping behaviors.

**Severity Tiers:**
| Points | Level | Implication |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| 0-39 | Low | Natural alignment; minimal intervention needed |
| 40-79 | Moderate | Noticeable gap; awareness and simple tools help |
| 80-139 | High | Significant gap; requires deliberate bridge-building |
| 140+ | Significant | Extreme gap; requires structural intervention |

**Research Basis:**
- Supplementary vs. Complementary Fit (Muchinsky & Monahan, 1987)
- Behavioral similarity research shows both benefits (communication ease) and costs (blind spots)
- The 40-point threshold maps to 2x observable behavioral difference — requiring both parties to significantly adapt

**Ground Truth Anchor:**
Daniel vs. Sareya = 236 points = Significant
- Daniel Natural: D=81, I=99, S=46, C=25
- Sareya Natural: D=11, I=60, S=99, C=99
- Calculation: |81-11| + |99-60| + |46-99| + |25-99| = 70 + 39 + 53 + 74 = 236

#### Passion Friction (CALCULABLE)

The values gap between two people across all seven dimensions.

**Calculation:**
```
PassionFriction = |A.Aesthetic - B.Aesthetic| + |A.Economic - B.Economic| +
                  |A.Individualistic - B.Individualistic| + |A.Political - B.Political| +
                  |A.Altruistic - B.Altruistic| + |A.Regulatory - B.Regulatory| +
                  |A.Theoretical - B.Theoretical|
```

**Per-Dimension Thresholds:**
| Gap | Level | Meaning |
|-----|-------|---------|
| 0-19 | Low | Similar priorities in this dimension |
| 20-39 | Moderate | Different but manageable |
| 40+ | High | Fundamental difference in what matters |

**Why "Calculable" Not "Confirmed":**

The gap is real math on two measured profiles. But values friction requires CONTEXT — two people can have a 50-point Economic gap and never experience friction if their roles don't require them to make resource decisions together.

**Research Basis:**
- Values congruence research (Meglino & Ravlin, 1998)
- Shared values predict team cohesion (r = 0.37) and reduced conflict (r = -0.31)
- Deep-level diversity (including values) has stronger long-term effects than surface-level diversity (Harrison et al., 1998)

#### Process Friction (TWO VARIABLES REQUIRED)

The thinking style gap between two people, measured through BOTH score gap AND bias comparison.

**Variable 1: Score Gap**
| Gap | Level |
|-----|-------|
| 0-1.9 | Low |
| 2.0-3.9 | Moderate |
| 4.0+ | High |

**Variable 2: Bias Comparison**
| Comparison | Result | Meaning |
|------------|--------|---------|
| (+) vs (-) | CONFLICT | Fundamental friction — opposite directions |
| (+) vs (=) or (-) vs (=) | TENSION | Different but not opposite |
| Same bias | ALIGNED | Natural alignment |

**Take the more severe result per dimension.**

**Apply to External Attributes:**
- Empathy (Heart)
- Practical Thinking (Hand)
- Systems Judgment (Head)

**Apply to Internal Attributes SEPARATELY:**
- Self-Esteem
- Role Awareness
- Self-Direction

**Why Both Variables:**

Score measures capacity development. Bias measures directional relationship. Two people can have identical capacity but pull in opposite directions. One variable captures half the picture.

**Research Basis:**
- Cognitive style research (Kirton, 1976; Sternberg, 1997)
- Thinking style diversity in teams shows curvilinear effects — some diversity helps, extreme diversity hurts
- Bias comparison maps to "approach vs. avoidance" orientation in self-determination theory (Deci & Ryan, 1985)

#### Internal Impact Friction (SEPARATE CONSTRUCT)

Dyadic comparison of Internal Attributes (Self-Esteem, Role Awareness, Self-Direction).

**Must be labeled and calculated separately from External Process Friction.**

Two people with opposite Self-Direction biases will clash on autonomy expectations. Two people with opposite Role Awareness biases will clash on boundary definitions. These are distinct from External processing conflicts.

### Critical Rules for Level 2

1. **Three dimensions, kept separate.** Never collapse Preference, Passion, and Process into a composite score.
2. **Natural scores for DISC.** Never use Adaptive.
3. **Both variables for Process.** Never calculate with score gap only or bias only.
4. **Internal and External separate.** Never merge into single Process score.

## LEVEL 3: TEAM

### Question
What are the systemic friction patterns across this team?

### Products
- **Team Insights** — Aggregate view of team dynamics
- **KRI Dashboard** — Key Retention Indicators
- **Priority Friction Areas** — Top 5 friction points ranked
- **Culture Visibility Model** — DISC × Values distribution

### Constructs

#### Team Friction Aggregation

Team friction is dyadic math applied across ALL pairwise combinations, then aggregated by dimension.

**Calculation Process:**
1. Calculate Level 2 dyadic friction for every pair (n people = n(n-1)/2 pairs)
2. Aggregate by dimension:
   - Which DISC dimensions create systemic friction across the team?
   - Which Values dimensions collide most?
   - Which Process dimensions show bias conflicts?
3. Rank Priority Friction Areas across all pairs and dimensions

**Output:**
- Dimension-level team friction scores
- Distribution of pair-level friction (how many High vs. Moderate vs. Low)
- Priority list of specific friction points requiring attention

**Research Basis:**
- Team composition research (Bell, 2007)
- Diversity-performance relationship is moderated by task type and team processes
- Process-focused interventions improve diverse team performance (Jehn et al., 1999)

#### Key Retention Indicators (KRI)

Aggregate Internal Attributes scores as leading indicators of retention risk.

**The Three Indicators:**
| Indicator | What Strain Looks Like | Risk Threshold |
|-----------|------------------------|----------------|
| Self-Esteem | Team undervalues themselves, doesn't advocate | 40%+ with (-) bias OR avg < 6.0 |
| Role Awareness | Unclear ownership, boundary confusion | 40%+ with (-) bias OR avg < 6.0 |
| Self-Direction | Needs clarity not getting, defaults to inaction | 40%+ with (-) bias OR avg < 6.0 |

**KRI is NOT friction.** It measures internal environment health, not relational gaps.

KRI connects to friction data (high friction with leader may drive negative internal attributes) but uses its own calculation logic based on team-wide distribution of Internal Attribute biases.

**Research Basis:**
- Person-Environment Fit and turnover intention (Tett & Meyer, 1993)
- Meta-analysis: P-E misfit predicts turnover intention (ρ = 0.29)
- Self-efficacy (Bandura, 1977) maps to Self-Esteem; role clarity (Rizzo et al., 1970) maps to Role Awareness; autonomy (Deci & Ryan, 1985) maps to Self-Direction
- All three are established predictors of job satisfaction and retention

#### Frustrated Practical Thinking Flag

**Special Case:** When a team member shows (-) bias on Practical Thinking in Internal Attributes, their environment has taught them that getting results doesn't matter.

This is the **strongest damage signal** in the entire assessment. It maps to "learned helplessness" (Seligman, 1975) — the person has learned that their efforts don't produce outcomes.

Flag separately in KRI Dashboard. Requires direct intervention.

### Critical Rules for Level 3

1. **Aggregate from Level 2.** Never calculate team friction directly from individual assessments — always through dyadic pairs.
2. **Keep KRI separate.** It's a retention risk indicator, not a friction score.
3. **Dimension-level rollup.** Show which specific dimensions create systemic patterns, not just overall team friction.

## LEVEL 4: ORGANIZATIONAL / LEADER

### Question
How do friction patterns ripple across teams and culture?

### Products
- **Love Where You Lead** — Leadership environment dashboard
- **Leader Environment Report** — Leader's style vs. what their teams need
- **Culture Visibility Model** — DISC × Values at organizational scale
- **Cross-Team Friction Analysis** — Patterns across multiple teams

### Constructs

#### Leader-Team Friction

The gap between a leader's style and what their direct reports collectively need.

**Calculation:**
1. Calculate Leader vs. each team member (Level 2 dyadic)
2. Aggregate to show:
   - Which dimensions show consistent friction across multiple team members?
   - Where does the leader's style create systemic cost?
   - Which team members experience the most friction with the leader?

**This is NOT the same as:**
- Leader's individual Preference Tax (Level 1)
- Team friction among members (Level 3)

It is specifically the leader's impact on their team, aggregated from dyadic calculations.

**Research Basis:**
- Leader-Member Exchange theory (Graen & Uhl-Bien, 1995)
- LMX quality predicts team performance (r = 0.30) and member satisfaction (r = 0.50)
- Leader behavioral flexibility research shows leaders who adapt style to member needs get better outcomes

#### Cross-Team Patterns

When a leader oversees multiple teams, patterns may emerge:
- Consistent friction on certain dimensions across all teams
- Specific teams with elevated friction
- Culture-level gaps between leader expectations and team norms

**Calculation:**
1. Calculate Level 3 team aggregations for each team
2. Compare across teams
3. Identify which patterns are leader-driven vs. team-composition-driven

#### Culture Visibility Model

DISC × Values distribution at organizational scale.

**Shows:**
- Are certain DISC styles over/under-represented?
- Are certain Values over/under-represented?
- What "type" does the organization reward/attract?
- Where are the blind spots?

**Research Basis:**
- Organizational culture research (Schneider, 1987)
- Attraction-Selection-Attrition (ASA) model predicts organizations become homogeneous over time
- Culture fit vs. culture add debate — both similarity and diversity have roles

### Critical Rules for Level 4

1. **Aggregate from Level 3.** Never calculate organizational friction directly from individuals — always through team-level aggregations.
2. **Separate leader impact from peer dynamics.** Leader-team friction and team-member friction are different constructs.
3. **Culture-level analysis requires sufficient data.** Minimum viable: 3+ teams, 15+ people assessed.

# PART III: PROHIBITED PATTERNS

## What Must Never Appear in Production

| Pattern | Why It's Wrong |
|---------|----------------|
| **Composite score combining Preference + Passion + Process** | Collapses confirmed data and signals into one number; not defensible |
| **Percentage normalization (/ 400, / 700, / 30)** | No scientific basis; denominators appear nowhere in validated methodology |
| **Process friction with only score gap** | Misses bias conflicts; captures half the picture |
| **Process friction with only bias comparison** | Misses capacity differences; captures half the picture |
| **Internal + External Attributes merged** | Different factor structures; psychologically distinct constructs |
| **KRI merged into friction score** | Different constructs measuring different things |
| **Adaptive DISC scores for friction calculation** | Friction lives in natural preferences, not current coping |
| **Individual-level methodology applied to dyadic** | Different questions, different constructs, different calculations |
| **Dyadic pairs summed directly to organization level** | Must aggregate through team level first |
