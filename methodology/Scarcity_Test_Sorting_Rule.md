# The Scarcity Test (Both-High Sorting Rule)

**Status:** Reference rule for Stage 02. Sorts both-high cases into competition or whose-standard.
**Date:** June 5, 2026
**Sits under:** Friction_Definition.md. This is the sorting rule the foundation pointed to but did not define.

---

## What this resolves

When two people both score high on the same trait, the score shows shared intensity, not which kind of friction it is. This rule sorts the both-high case into competition or whose-standard, using one test, and names where the answer comes from so it is never left to interpretation.

---

## Definition of "only one can hold it"

A target is "only one can hold it" when it is a single assignable unit, so giving it to one person denies it to the other at the same moment.

The non-interpretive test, a yes or no: can both people be fully satisfied on this target at the same time, without one taking from the other?

No, one's gain is the other's loss. Only one can hold it. This is competition.

Yes, both can be fully satisfied at once. Both can hold it. This is whose-standard.

The test is whether the target subtracts, not how badly anyone wants it. Control of one decision subtracts, if I hold the call you do not. Order does not subtract, my order does not use up your order.

---

## How we determine it

Scarcity is not in the score. Whether a target subtracts is a fact about the target and the situation, never about the person's number. The assessment can tell you both people score high. It cannot tell you whether the thing they want is a single unit. That is the input the methodology was missing, and the reason the question kept coming back.

The answer comes from two places, neither of them the score.

**The trait's target object gives the default.** Look at what the trait drives toward. If the target is a position, a decision, a slot, or a pool, it is a unit, only one can hold it. If the target is a quality or condition, like order, beauty, correctness, or efficiency, both can hold it.

**The shared demand confirms the case.** A unit only causes competition if the two people actually share it. Two high-D leaders running separate teams do not compete for control, there is no single call they both own. Put them on one joint decision and the unit is shared, now they compete. This is the confirmation input for competition.

---

## Default sort by trait

| Trait | What it drives toward | Unit or quality | Default sort |
|---|---|---|---|
| D | Control of the decision, the final call | Unit | Competition |
| I | Attention, the spotlight | Unit-ish, finite | Competition-prone, weaker |
| S | Steady pace, stability | Quality | Rarely fires |
| C | The correct process | Quality | Whose-standard |
| Political | The leadership seat, and the right way to lead | Unit and quality | Dual-source |
| Individualistic | Being the distinct one | Unit-ish, limited room | Competition-prone, weaker |
| Regulatory | Order | Quality | Whose-standard |
| Aesthetic | Form, beauty | Quality | Whose-standard |
| Economic | The efficient path, or a shared budget | Quality, unless one pool | Whose-standard, dual if shared pool |
| Altruist | Serving others | Quality | Bond, not rivalrous |
| Theoretical | Truth, knowledge | Quality | Whose-standard or bond |

---

## The case rule

A competition default fires as live friction only when a real shared demand puts the unit in contention, one joint decision, one role both want, one pool both draw from. Separate domains mean the unit is not shared, so competition does not fire even at both-high.

---

## The dual-source edge

A few traits carry both. Two high-Political people can fight over the one leadership seat, which is scarce, and over the right way to lead, which is not. When a trait does both, do not force one bucket. Tag it with both sources, route the scarce part to allocation and the standard part to adjudication. Expect a small number of dual-source traits, not a forced single label on every one.

---

## The chain, stated once

Scores flag both-high. The target object sorts the kind, unit means competition, quality means whose-standard. The shared demand confirms it is live, a unit only competes if the two people actually share it. Both-high is the signal. The target object and the shared demand are what turn it into a verdict.

Solve follows the sort. Scarce things divide, so competition is solved by allocation. Standards do not divide, because order is not a quantity to split, so whose-standard is solved by adjudication. Sorting by scarcity is what makes the downstream bridge correct.

---

## Honest uncertainty

The default table is a reasoned read of each trait's target object, not a validated mapping. Stress-test it against real pairs.

The weaker calls are I and Individualistic, where the target is finite but not strictly a single unit, so competition is real but softer. Economic is dual only when the two people draw from one shared pool, otherwise it is a quality and sorts to whose-standard. Treat these as signal until production pairs confirm them.
