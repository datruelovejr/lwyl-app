# How We Define Friction

**Status:** Foundation document. Source of truth for what friction is and where it comes from.
**Date:** June 5, 2026
**Governs:** The friction methodology, the validator skill, and the app.

---

## What friction is

Friction is the cost that appears when two trait profiles meet a shared demand.

Three parts matter. It takes two profiles, so friction is always between named people, never a single person's score. It needs a shared demand, a decision, a task, a role, something they both face. And it is a cost, the energy, time, or quality lost when those two profiles meet that demand.

Friction is trait-derived and stable. It does not move when people try harder. What moves is the realized cost of the friction, measured through behavioral adherence and Goal Attainment Scaling, not by re-scoring the friction itself.

---

## Where friction comes from

Friction arises in two families. A family is a group of friction types that share the same root cause, which means they share the same fix. The split exists so the system always reaches for the right solve.

### Family one: Approach-clash

The cost comes from people experiencing each other's way as wrong. This family is driven by EBIB. Each person's lens, built from their environment, beliefs, identity, and proven methods, makes their own way feel right and a different way feel wrong. The friction is a fight about how.

Two types live here.

**Difference.** The two people have different trait levels, so their approaches differ, and each lens reads the other as wrong. A high-D and a low-D clash over pace, one believes fast action is right, the other believes deliberate analysis is right.

**Whose-standard-wins.** The two people are the same on the trait, both high, but they hold different specific versions of the right way, so they clash over whose standard governs. Two people who both value order argue about whose system is the correct one.

The solve is lens reconciliation. Translation for difference, helping each see the other's lens. Set the Standard for whose-standard, agreeing on one standard or scoping each person to a domain where their standard governs.

### Family two: Structural

The cost comes from how the traits sit against a scarce resource or an unmet demand, not from anyone's lens. No one thinks anyone is wrong. The friction is a problem with the setup.

Two types live here.

**Competition.** The two people are both high and both reach for the same finite thing, control, the lead, the spotlight. The clash is not about whose way is right. Two people with the identical approach still fight over who holds the one role.

**Coverage-gap.** The two people are both low on a needed capacity, so a demand goes uncovered. They agree with each other. The cost is the gap, not a clash between them.

The solve is structural. Split Ownership for competition, carving up the scarce thing so each owns a piece. Create Systems, Hire Talent, or Delegate for coverage-gap, building a complement around the missing capacity.

## The map

| Family | Type | What it is | Root cause | Solve |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Approach-clash | Difference | Different trait levels, clashing approaches | EBIB, each lens reads the other as wrong | Translation |
| Approach-clash | Whose-standard-wins | Same high trait, different version of right | EBIB, each lens reads the other as wrong | Set the Standard |
| Structural | Competition | Both high, both want the same finite thing | The setup, one scarce resource | Split Ownership |
| Structural | Coverage-gap | Both low on a needed capacity | The setup, an unmet demand | Create Systems, Hire Talent, Delegate |

## The four fix names, defined in plain words

The fixes used to carry I/O Psychology terms, allocation and adjudication. Those words are gone from the build. Plain names replace them so no one has to guess. Here is what each fix means and when it fires.

**Translation.** Help each person see the other's way. Use it when two people work differently and each reads the other as wrong. You are not picking a winner. You are getting them to understand each other. This is the fix for Difference.

**Set the Standard.** Decide whose rule the team follows. Use it when two people both care about the same thing, like order or quality, but hold different versions of the right way. You can both have order at once, so the problem is not the thing, it is whose rule governs. You either pick one rule for the shared space, or give each person a lane where their rule rules. This is the fix for Whose-standard-wins. It replaces the old word adjudication.

**Split Ownership.** Divide the one thing both people want. Use it when there is only one of something, one seat, one final call, one budget, and both reach for it. Nobody is wrong, there is just one of it. You carve it up so each person owns a real piece. This is the fix for Competition. It replaces the old word allocation.

**Create Systems, Hire Talent, or Delegate.** Build a complement around a hole. Use it when two people, or a team, are both low on a capacity the work needs, and no one covers it. They agree with each other, so there is nothing to reconcile. You build a system to remove the need, hire someone who has the capacity, or hand the work to someone who does. This is the fix for Coverage-gap.

**One thing to know about the rigor skill.** The validator skill still uses the old words allocation and adjudication inside its own engine, because that is its I/O Psychology language. They mean exactly Split Ownership and Set the Standard in this order. The build, the app, and every stage artifact use the plain names. The skill is the only place the old words remain, and this box is the bridge between them.

## The rule that prevents the old contradiction

The system used to say friction comes from difference. That was an overreach. EBIB explains the approach-clash family only. It is not the theory of all friction.

So two rules hold, always.

EBIB applies to the approach-clash family. It explains why difference and whose-standard generate friction, and it powers the bridges that resolve them.

EBIB never applies to the structural family. Telling two people who both want control to understand each other's perspective does nothing, because they already share the perspective. They need the role carved up. Applying lens-understanding to a structural problem gives wrong advice. This is the foundational error that broke earlier build attempts, where the app treated lens-understanding as the universal solve.

Saying same-style friction exists is not a contradiction, because same-style friction is structural, and EBIB never claimed it.

---

## Honest uncertainty

The placement of whose-standard-wins in the approach-clash family is a reasoned call, not a validated one. Its clash is EBIB, but its solve carries a structural piece, scoping domains. Stress-test this placement in the methodology build rather than treating it as settled.

The two-family split is a conceptual architecture grounded in the mechanisms, difference in root cause and difference in solve. It is defensible and it removes the contradiction, and it should still be challenged in Stage 00 before the rest of the method is built on it.
