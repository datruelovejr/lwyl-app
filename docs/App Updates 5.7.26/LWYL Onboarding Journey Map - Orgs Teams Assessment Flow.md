# LWYL Onboarding Journey Map: Orgs/Teams Innermetrix Assessment Flow

## The Full End-to-End Journey - All 5 Engagement Layers Applied

This document maps the complete user experience from the moment an organization signs up through assessment completion and first app activation - with every step evaluated against the 5-layer engagement architecture, Jesse Cole's 5 Es, and Coleman's First 100 Days emotional lifecycle.

---

## THE HYBRID ASSESSMENT MODEL

The current flow is a hybrid: the admin triggers the assessment process inside the app, but there's still a manual step with Innermetrix to generate/distribute the actual assessment link. This creates both an opportunity and a risk - the opportunity is that the app becomes the command center for the entire team onboarding; the risk is that the manual Innermetrix handoff introduces friction and delay right at the moment where momentum matters most.

---

## PHASE 1: ORG CREATION & ADMIN SETUP

**Coleman Phase:** Assess → Admit (the org buyer is evaluating and then committing)

**What happens:** An organizational buyer (HR leader, executive, team lead) decides to bring BTCG/LWYL to their team. They create an organization inside the app.

### The 5 Layers at This Step

**Layer 1 - Habit Loop:** This is the first trigger. The admin's internal trigger is the pain they're trying to solve - team friction, communication breakdown, retention issues. The app needs to immediately validate that trigger. The action must be effortless: creating an org should take under 2 minutes. The variable reward is the first glimpse of what's coming - a preview of what their team dashboard will look like once assessments are complete. The investment is the org itself - they've now created something inside the system.

**Layer 2 - Motivation Design:** Autonomy is critical here. The admin should feel like they're building something, not filling out a form. Let them name the org, set the context ("What's the biggest challenge your team faces right now?"), and choose how they want to roll this out. Competence comes from the system making them feel smart - confirm their choice with language like "You're setting up something most organizations never build: a behavioral intelligence system for your team." Relatedness is the connection to their team - every step should reference the people who will benefit.

**Layer 3 - Behavioral Mechanics:** Endowed Progress is the big one. The moment the org is created, show them they've already completed Step 1 of a 4-step setup process: (1) Create your org ✓, (2) Add your team, (3) Send assessments, (4) Unlock your team dashboard. They didn't start at zero. They started at 25%. The Zeigarnik Effect kicks in immediately - they have an open loop. The dashboard is waiting but incomplete. Commitment and Consistency: asking "What's the biggest challenge your team faces?" gets a micro-commitment that anchors their reason for being here.

**Layer 4 - Experience Design:** The Peak-End Rule matters even at this administrative step. The peak should be the moment they see the preview of what's coming - a sample team dashboard or a brief animation showing what their team insights will look like. Don't make org creation feel like a settings page. Make it feel like the first step of a transformation. The end should be empowering: "Your team's leadership intelligence system is live. Now let's bring your people in." Coleman's Affirm phase is active here - the buyer just committed. They need immediate confirmation that this was the right call. Don't let them sit in silence after creating the org.

**Layer 5 - Service Design:** This is where Daniel's voice matters. The org creation experience should feel like Daniel is guiding them through it, not like a SaaS onboarding wizard. Micro-copy should sound like a trusted advisor: "Let's get your team set up" not "Complete your organization profile." Every loading state should feel intentional: "Building your team's home base..." not a spinning wheel.

### Jesse Cole's 5 Es Applied

**Eliminate Friction:** Org creation should require the absolute minimum: org name, admin email, and estimated team size. Everything else can come later. Don't ask for billing, don't ask for detailed company info, don't ask for anything that slows down the momentum between "I want to do this" and "I'm doing this." Like Jesse's all-inclusive ticket - remove every barrier between the decision and the experience.

**Entertain Always:** Even administrative setup can have personality. A welcome message that breaks the expected pattern: "Most organizations talk about team culture. You're about to actually see yours." This is the Savannah Bananas naming their team after a fruit - the unexpected creates attention.

**Experiment Constantly:** This step should be A/B tested relentlessly. Test whether showing a sample dashboard during setup increases completion. Test whether asking the "biggest challenge" question increases retention at Day 7. Test whether a 60-second video from Daniel during org creation changes activation rates.

## PHASE 2: TEAM MEMBER ADDITION

**Coleman Phase:** Activate (the admin is taking their first real action inside the system)

**What happens:** The admin adds team members to the organization. This could be manual entry (name + email) or bulk upload. Each team member added becomes a person who will receive an Innermetrix assessment link.

### The 5 Layers at This Step

**Layer 1 - Habit Loop:** The trigger is the momentum from Phase 1 - the open loop of the incomplete dashboard. The action is adding team members, which should be dead simple: type a name, type an email, hit enter. Or paste a list. Or upload a CSV. Three paths, all effortless. The variable reward is watching the team roster build in real time - each person added should feel like the dashboard is getting closer to alive. The investment is significant: every name added is stored value. The admin is building something that didn't exist before.

**Layer 2 - Motivation Design:** Autonomy means the admin chooses how to add people - one by one, bulk, CSV. Don't force a single path. Competence means the system confirms progress: "4 of 6 team members added." Relatedness is the emotional weight of seeing your team's names on screen - this isn't abstract data, these are real people the admin works with every day. Lean into that: show names, not just email addresses. Make it feel human.

**Layer 3 - Behavioral Mechanics:** Goal Gradient Effect activates here. As the admin adds each team member, the progress bar toward "Send Assessments" accelerates. If they said their team is 6 people, adding the 5th person should feel like they're almost there. Endowed Progress continues: they're now at Step 2 of 4. Loss Aversion starts building: they've invested time adding these names. Abandoning now means losing that work. The Zeigarnik Effect deepens: the team dashboard preview should update with placeholder silhouettes for each person added, making the incomplete picture more vivid and the pull to complete it stronger.

**Layer 4 - Experience Design:** The peak moment here is seeing the team take shape visually. Don't just show a list of names - show them in a team visualization (even if it's placeholder). The moment the admin sees their team represented as a group for the first time is an Elevation moment (Power of Moments). The end of this step should preview what's next with genuine excitement: "Your team is set. When they complete their assessments, this is where the magic happens." Show them the dashboard one more time - but this time with their actual team members' names in the placeholder slots.

**Layer 5 - Service Design:** The micro-copy matters enormously. When they add "Sarah Johnson" - the system should say "Sarah's been added to your team" not "Contact added successfully." Use first names. Make it feel like you're welcoming real people, not processing records. If Daniel has already met these team members (Tier 1 context), the system could reference that: "Sarah's already part of the journey."

### Jesse Cole's 5 Es Applied

**Eliminate Friction:** Don't require team members to have accounts before being added. Don't require job titles, departments, or any other field that slows things down. Name and email. That's it. Everything else comes from the assessment or can be added later. Jesse's principle: "Stop doing all the things that our customers hate."

**Engage Deeply:** This is where "do for one what you wish you could do for many" applies. Each team member added represents a real relationship the admin cares about. The system should honor that. Consider: after adding all team members, surface one brief prompt - "Which team relationship are you most curious about?" This is both a micro-commitment (Layer 3) and a deep engagement moment. The admin is already thinking about specific people.

## PHASE 3: ASSESSMENT LINK GENERATION & DISTRIBUTION

**Coleman Phase:** Affirm → Activate (this is the HIGHEST RISK transition in the entire journey)

**What happens:** The admin triggers the Innermetrix assessment process inside the app. Because this is a hybrid model, there's a manual step - either the admin gets a link from the system/from Daniel that needs to be shared with team members, or the system generates individual links that get distributed via email. Either way, assessments go out and the admin waits for completions.

### Why This Is the Most Dangerous Step

This is where Coleman's research is most relevant: 20-70% of newly acquired customers stop doing business within 100 days due to feeling neglected. The gap between "I sent assessments" and "My team completed them" is a dead zone. The admin has momentum, excitement, and commitment - and then they hit a wall of waiting. Every day a team member hasn't completed their assessment, the admin's doubt grows: "Did I make the right call? Is my team going to take this seriously? Is this going to work?"

This is Jesse Cole's mirror moment applied to the onboarding process itself. Put yourself in the admin's shoes during this waiting period. What are the friction points? Uncertainty. Lack of control. No visibility. Silence from the system.

### The 5 Layers at This Step

**Layer 1 - Habit Loop:** The trigger challenge is real: there's no natural internal trigger during the waiting period. The system must create external triggers that are genuinely useful, not nagging. Send the admin updates when each team member completes their assessment: "[Name] just completed their assessment. 3 of 6 done." This serves double duty - it's a trigger to return to the app AND it's a variable reward (who completed? what will their data show?). The action on return should be viewing the completion tracker. The investment is watching their team dashboard slowly come to life.

**Layer 2 - Motivation Design:** Autonomy means giving the admin tools to manage the process: the ability to resend reminders, check who's completed, see who hasn't started. They should feel in control, not helpless. Competence means the system educates them on what to expect: "Most teams complete all assessments within 5-7 days. Here's what you can do to speed things up." Relatedness means framing completions in human terms: "Sarah completed her assessment" not "1 of 6 assessments completed."

**Layer 3 - Behavioral Mechanics:** The Zeigarnik Effect is doing heavy lifting here. The incomplete dashboard is the open loop that keeps the admin checking back. Make it vivid: show team member cards that are "locked" until their assessment completes - dark silhouettes that brighten and reveal when data arrives. This is Netflix's auto-play preview for the next episode applied to assessment completions. Loss Aversion: remind the admin of what they've already built - "Your org is set up, your team is loaded, 3 assessments are in. You're 50% of the way to unlocking your full team dashboard." Endowed Progress: show them a progress bar that started at 25% (org created) and is now at 60% or 70%. Goal Gradient: when 4 of 6 have completed, the pull to get those last 2 should feel almost irresistible.

**Layer 4 - Experience Design:** Each assessment completion should be a mini peak moment. Not just a notification - a small celebration: "Marcus just unlocked his profile. Here's a 10-second preview of what you'll see when your full team is ready." This serves multiple purposes: it rewards the admin's patience, it previews the value (Zeigarnik), and it creates a Peak-End moment for that specific notification interaction. Coleman's Affirm phase demands active reassurance during this waiting period. The system should proactively reach out at Day 2 and Day 5 if assessments aren't complete. Not nagging - affirming: "Teams that complete their assessments within the first week see the biggest impact. Your team is on track."

**Layer 5 - Service Design:** This is where the human layer matters most. Daniel should personally reach out to the admin during this phase if it's a high-value engagement. Even a 30-second voice message: "Hey [Name], I see your team is halfway through their assessments. When they're done, you're going to see your team in a way you never have before. Can't wait to walk through it with you." The Ritz-Carlton principle: the $2,000 rule applied to time. Invest disproportionate personal attention during the highest-risk moment.

### Jesse Cole's 5 Es Applied

**Eliminate Friction (CRITICAL):** The assessment distribution process itself needs to be as frictionless as possible for team MEMBERS, not just the admin. The email they receive should be warm, brief, and clear. It should take no more than 15-20 minutes to complete. The link should work on mobile. There should be zero account creation required before taking the assessment. Every barrier between "I got this email" and "I finished the assessment" is a place where the admin's investment dies. Jesse's Ticketmaster story applies directly: they eliminated ticket fees because every fee was a friction point that pushed people away. Every step between the team member receiving the link and completing the assessment is a "fee" that threatens the admin's experience.

**Entertain Always:** The assessment completion notifications should have personality. Not just "Assessment complete" but something that builds anticipation: "Another piece of the puzzle just fell into place. [Name]'s profile is ready and waiting." This is Jesse's principle of entertaining at every touchpoint - even invoices, even voicemail, even the moments people expect to be boring.

## PHASE 4: ASSESSMENT COMPLETION - THE DASHBOARD UNLOCKS

**Coleman Phase:** Accomplish (the admin achieves their first goal - seeing their team)

**What happens:** All team members (or a critical mass) have completed their assessments. The team dashboard goes from placeholder to populated. The admin sees their team's behavioral data for the first time inside the LWYL app.

### Why This Must Be the Biggest Peak in the Entire Journey

This is the moment the admin has been working toward since Phase 1. Every step - creating the org, adding team members, distributing assessments, waiting - has been building toward this reveal. If this moment is flat, everything that came before loses its meaning retroactively. The Peak-End Rule says people judge the entire experience based on the peak and the ending. This IS the peak.

Jesse Cole's entire philosophy converges here. This is the first game at the ballpark. This is the moment the fans rush in. The Savannah Bananas didn't just play baseball - they created an experience people would drive 40 hours for. The dashboard reveal needs to be that caliber of experience within the context of a leadership intelligence app.

### The 5 Layers at This Step

**Layer 1 - Habit Loop:** This is where the internal trigger gets wired. The admin sees their team in a way they've never seen them before. The emotion that surfaces - recognition, surprise, understanding, even relief - becomes the internal trigger that brings them back. "I wonder what else is in there." "I need to look at [specific team member] again before our 1-on-1 on Tuesday." The variable reward must be extraordinary on this first visit: surface the single most surprising insight about their team composition. Not a data dump. ONE insight that makes them say "Oh. That's why." The investment is immediate: they're going to start exploring, saving notes, drilling into individual profiles. Every click stores value.

**Layer 2 - Motivation Design:** Competence peaks here. The admin feels like they've accomplished something meaningful - they now understand their team in a way they didn't before. The language should name that growth: "You now have leadership intelligence that most executives never access." Autonomy means letting them explore freely - don't force a guided tour through the data. Let them click what interests them. Some will go straight to the team member they have the most friction with. Some will look at the big picture first. Let them drive. Relatedness is inherent: every data point connects to a real person they lead.

**Layer 3 - Behavioral Mechanics:** Endowed Progress completes its first arc: "Step 4 of 4 - Your team dashboard is live." But immediately open the next arc: "Your team intelligence is ready. Here's what most leaders explore first..." This is critical - the old progress bar closes and a new one opens. Don't leave the admin in a state of completion without a next step. The Zeigarnik Effect shifts from "will my team complete their assessments?" to "what else can I learn about my team?" Open new loops: "Your team has one Values alignment that's nearly perfect - and one that explains most of your tension." Show them enough to hook them, then let them discover the rest. Investment is massive: every insight they absorb, every note they save, every team member profile they explore increases stored value and switching costs.

**Layer 4 - Experience Design:** This is where you engineer the EPIC moment. Elevation: the dashboard should feel like a reveal, not a page load. Consider a brief build animation where team members' profiles light up one by one around a central view. Something that breaks the pattern of every other SaaS dashboard they've ever seen. Insight: the first interpretive statement should land with emotional weight - "You lead a team with 60% High S members. You're a High D/I. That gap is where most of your friction lives - and it's not anyone's fault." Pride: acknowledge what they've done to get here - "You've built something most leaders never create: a living picture of how your team actually works." Connection: tie it to their people - "The insights you see here represent real patterns in real relationships. Every data point is a person who trusts you to lead them well."

The END of this first dashboard session matters as much as the peak. Don't let them drift away. When they move to close or navigate away, surface a closing statement: "You've taken the first step. The more you explore, the sharper these insights become. See you next time." Peak-End Rule: the close is warm, empowering, forward-looking.

**Layer 5 - Service Design:** Daniel should know the moment the dashboard unlocks. If he's actively consulting with this org, he should reach out within 24 hours: "I saw your team's dashboard went live. Before we meet, spend 10 minutes just exploring. Pay attention to what surprises you. That's where we'll start." This is Apple's Steps of Service: Approach (reach out), Probe (ask what surprised them), Present (guide them to deeper insights in the next session), Listen (let them tell you what they noticed), End (close with a single empowering observation about their team).

### Jesse Cole's 5 Es Applied

**Engage Deeply:** This is "do for one what you wish you could do for many." If the system can surface one deeply personal insight for each admin - not generic, but specific to their team's unique composition - that's the moment they become fans, not customers. Jesse's story about the engaged couple at the Bananas game applies: listen carefully (their team data IS the listening), respond creatively (surface the insight that matters most for THIS specific team).

**Empower Action:** The dashboard should make the admin feel like they can DO something with what they see. Not just observe - act. "Based on your team's composition, here's one thing you can try in your next team meeting." This is Jesse's final E: stop standing still, start standing out. Give them a specific, small, immediate action that bridges insight to behavior.

## PHASE 5: TEAM MEMBER FIRST APP EXPERIENCE

**Coleman Phase:** Activate → Acclimate (team members are entering the system for the first time)

**What happens:** Team members who completed the Innermetrix assessment now get access to the LWYL app. They see their own profile data and, depending on the org's settings, may see team-level insights or other members' profiles.

### The 5 Layers at This Step

**Layer 1 - Habit Loop:** The team member's internal trigger is different from the admin's. They're curious about themselves: "What does my assessment say about me?" The app should deliver on that curiosity in the first 60 seconds. Surface their top-level profile with ONE surprising insight they didn't get from the standard Innermetrix report. The variable reward is the reframe: their data presented through the LWYL lens, which connects personality to leadership behavior in ways the raw report doesn't. Investment begins immediately: the moment they save a reflection, set an intention, or explore a team member's profile, they've stored value.

**Layer 2 - Motivation Design:** Autonomy means they choose what to explore. Don't force them through their own profile first - some will want to see the team immediately. Competence means every screen teaches them something: "Your High D style means you process decisions quickly. 70% of your team processes more slowly. That's not resistance - that's their operating speed." Relatedness connects them to their teammates: "You share the same top Values driver as 2 of your team members. Here's what that means for your working relationship."

**Layer 3 - Behavioral Mechanics:** Endowed Progress: "Your leadership profile is complete. You're already ahead." Show them they've accomplished something just by completing the assessment. Zeigarnik: "Your team's Connection Agreements will be available once your leader completes the Tier 2 journey" - an open loop that points toward the full system's value. Commitment and Consistency: ask them one question during onboarding - "What's one thing you wish your team understood about how you work?" Their answer becomes a commitment and a data point.

**Layer 4 - Experience Design:** The peak moment for team members is different from the admin's. It's the moment they see themselves accurately described - the "that's SO me" moment. Design for it. The Insight element of EPIC dominates: "You've always known you like structure. What you might not know is that your need for structure is in the top 15% of all leaders assessed - and that's not a limitation. It's a superpower in the right environment." The end should point toward the team: "Now that you know yourself through the LWYL lens, imagine what's possible when your whole team has this same understanding."

**Layer 5 - Service Design:** The team member experience should feel connected to the admin's experience and to Daniel's consulting. It shouldn't feel like a separate product. Language, tone, and design should be consistent. If Daniel has met this team member in a group session, the app should feel like a continuation of that conversation, not a cold start.

## PHASE 6: ADMIN RE-ENGAGEMENT & TIER 2 TRANSITION

**Coleman Phase:** Adopt → Advocate (the admin becomes a regular user and begins telling others)

**What happens:** The admin has been using the dashboard for days or weeks. They're now familiar with the basic insights. The question is: do they deepen engagement (Tier 2 individual journey, Connection Agreements) or do they plateau?

### The 5 Layers at This Step

**Layer 1 - Habit Loop:** External triggers shift from assessment-tracking to insight-surfacing. Monday morning: "This week's team insight is ready." Before a scheduled 1-on-1: "Heading into your meeting with Sarah? Here's what to keep in mind about her communication style." The trigger timing matters more than the content - prompt them when the behavioral context matches. The variable reward must keep rotating: different insights, different team members, different angles. If they see the same DISC overview every time, the habit dies.

**Layer 2 - Motivation Design:** Competence deepens through the Tier 2 journey's progressive complexity (Week 1: you, Week 2: your team, Week 3: the gap, Week 4: the bridge). This is Flow State design - the challenge-skill balance increases as the leader's understanding grows. Purpose enters the picture: "Building a team environment where people thrive isn't just management. It's legacy." Leaders who see the app as serving something larger than themselves will sustain engagement 50% longer (Dan Pink's research).

**Layer 3 - Behavioral Mechanics:** Loss Aversion is now the primary driver. They've invested weeks of exploration, saved notes, built understanding. Leaving means losing all of that. The Zeigarnik Effect shifts to the Tier 2 journey: "You've seen what your team looks like. In Week 3, you'll see why your strongest team member might be your biggest friction point." Goal Gradient accelerates as they move through Tier 2 - by Week 3, the pull toward completing the Connection Agreement in Week 4 should feel almost gravitational.

**Layer 4 - Experience Design:** Each Tier 2 week has a designed peak (from the Engagement Architecture document): Week 1 is Elevation (seeing yourself through the LWYL lens), Week 2 is Insight (team composition reveals hidden patterns), Week 3 is Insight + Connection (the gap reveal), Week 4 is Pride + Connection (the Connection Agreement). The end of the entire Tier 2 journey should be the biggest emotional moment in the product: the leader has built something - a documented understanding of how to lead their specific team. That's a Pride moment that becomes the foundation for Advocacy.

**Layer 5 - Service Design:** Daniel's consulting sessions should reference the app journey. "I see you're in Week 3 - the gap reveal. Let's talk about what came up for you." The app and the human relationship must feel like one continuous experience. This is the Service Profit Chain in action: Daniel's energy drives leader engagement, which drives team impact, which drives referrals.

## CRITICAL FRICTION POINTS & DESIGN RESPONSES

### Friction Point 1: The Innermetrix Handoff

**The problem:** The hybrid model means there's a moment where the admin has to leave the LWYL app experience and interact with the Innermetrix assessment system. This is a context switch that breaks immersion.

**The design response:** Make the LWYL app the single source of truth for the assessment process. Even if the actual assessment lives on Innermetrix, the admin should manage everything - invitations, tracking, reminders, completions - inside LWYL. The Innermetrix system should be invisible to the admin. They should never have to log into a separate platform, copy a link, or manually check completion status. Every one of those steps is a "convenience fee" that erodes the experience (Jesse Cole's Ticketmaster analogy).

**Framework alignment:** Fogg B=MAP says ability is the bottleneck. If sending assessments requires multiple steps across multiple systems, the behavior will fail regardless of motivation. Reduce the steps to one: "Send Assessments" button inside the LWYL app. Everything else happens backstage.

### Friction Point 2: The Waiting Period

**The problem:** Between sending assessments and getting completions, the admin has nothing to do in the app. Dead time kills momentum.

**The design response:** Fill the waiting period with value. Surface the admin's own profile insights while they wait. Offer a "While your team completes their assessments" mini-experience: "Let's explore your own leadership profile through the LWYL lens. When your team's data arrives, you'll already know what you're looking for." This serves triple duty: it gives the admin something to do (eliminates dead time), it pre-loads their understanding (endowed progress for the team reveal), and it creates investment (stored value from reflections and notes).

**Framework alignment:** This is the Savannah Bananas' pre-game experience. They don't wait for the first pitch to start entertaining. The parking penguins, the player introductions, the dance-offs - all of it happens before the game starts. The "game" for the admin is the team dashboard. But the experience starts the moment they create the org.

### Friction Point 3: Team Members Who Don't Complete Assessments

**The problem:** If 4 of 6 team members complete their assessments but 2 don't, the admin is stuck. The dashboard feels incomplete. The experience stalls.

**The design response:** Two strategies working together. First, don't hold the entire experience hostage to 100% completion. Unlock the dashboard with a configurable threshold (e.g., 60% or 4 of 6). Show what's available and mark what's pending: "4 of 6 profiles ready. Here's what your team looks like so far - and here's what changes when everyone's in." Second, give the admin easy tools to nudge the remaining members: one-tap reminders with pre-written, warm language. "Hey [Name], the team is almost there. Your assessment takes about 15 minutes and it'll give [Admin] the insights they need to lead us all better."

**Framework alignment:** Goal Gradient Effect: being at 4 of 6 creates acceleration energy. The system should amplify that: "You're 67% there. Two more assessments and your full team picture unlocks." Loss Aversion: "Your 4 completed profiles are waiting. Don't let them go stale - the insights are sharpest when the full team is represented."

### Friction Point 4: The Post-Dashboard-Reveal Plateau

**The problem:** The admin sees the dashboard, has their "aha" moment, and then... what? Without a clear next step, they'll visit once, be impressed, and never come back. The initial peak becomes the entire experience instead of the beginning of a sustained engagement.

**The design response:** The dashboard reveal MUST end with an open loop that pulls toward return. Specific strategies: (1) Surface one insight that's partially revealed - "Your team has a tension pattern we see in 1 of every 5 teams. Come back Thursday to see the full analysis." (2) Prompt an implementation intention: "When do you want to check your team insights this week? We'll remind you." (3) Preview the Tier 2 journey: "What you've seen today is the foundation. The 4-week deep dive shows you exactly how to lead this team differently." (4) Connect to a real upcoming event: "Your next team meeting is the perfect time to try one thing you learned today. We'll send you a prep insight 30 minutes before."

**Framework alignment:** This is the Zeigarnik Effect at its most critical application point. Netflix doesn't end an episode on resolution - it ends on a cliffhanger. The dashboard reveal should close one loop (you can see your team) while opening three more (but here's what you haven't seen yet, here's what to do about it, here's what changes when you go deeper).

## OPEN LOOPS TO ENGINEER THROUGHOUT THE JOURNEY

Open loops are the invisible architecture that keeps users returning. Here are the specific open loops to plant at each phase:

**Phase 1 (Org Creation):** "Your team dashboard is being prepared..." → Loop: what will my team look like?

**Phase 2 (Team Addition):** Show placeholder team visualization → Loop: what will this look like when it's filled in?

**Phase 3 (Assessment Distribution):** "When [Name] completes their assessment, you'll be the first to know what it reveals" → Loop: what will each person's profile show?

**Phase 3.5 (Each Completion):** "3 profiles ready. Your team is starting to take shape. The most interesting pattern usually emerges around the 4th or 5th profile..." → Loop: what's the pattern?

**Phase 4 (Dashboard Reveal):** "Your team has one Values alignment that's nearly perfect - and one that explains most of your tension" → Loop: which one is which?

**Phase 5 (Team Member Onboarding):** "Your leader is building a team intelligence system. When it's complete, you'll see how your strengths fit the bigger picture" → Loop: what's my role in the team composition?

**Phase 6 (Tier 2 Transition):** "You've seen what your team looks like. Next week, you'll see why your strongest team member might be your biggest friction point" → Loop: who is it? Why?

## PEAK MOMENTS TO ENGINEER

Not every moment needs to be a peak. But every phase needs at least one designed moment that the user will remember and potentially share.

**Phase 1 Peak:** The preview of the future dashboard. "This is what's coming for your team." Elevation through anticipation.

**Phase 2 Peak:** Seeing their team represented visually for the first time - even as placeholders. "Your team, together, in one view." Elevation + Connection.

**Phase 3 Peak:** Each individual assessment completion notification. "Marcus just unlocked his profile. Here's a preview..." Insight through anticipation.

**Phase 4 Peak (THE BIG ONE):** The full team reveal with the first interpretive insight. "You lead a High S team and you're a High D/I. That gap is where most of your friction lives - and it's not anyone's fault." Insight + Connection. This is the moment the admin becomes a fan, not a customer.

**Phase 5 Peak:** The team member's "that's SO me" moment when they see their profile through the LWYL lens. Insight + Pride.

**Phase 6 Peak:** The Week 3 gap reveal - "Do you love the place you lead, or do you love that you're the leader?" This is the confrontation moment that rewires their entire understanding. Insight at its deepest.

## COLEMAN'S FIRST 100 DAYS - MAPPED TO EACH PHASE

| Day Range | Coleman Phase | LWYL Moment | Emotional State | Design Priority |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Day 0 | Assess | First contact with BTCG/LWYL | Curiosity + Skepticism | Social proof, diagnostic hook |
| Day 0-1 | Admit | Org creation decision | Commitment + Hope | Immediate momentum |
| Day 1-3 | Affirm | Waiting for assessments | Doubt + Impatience | Active reassurance, admin profile exploration |
| Day 3-7 | Activate | First assessment completions | Excitement + Anticipation | Completion notifications as mini-peaks |
| Day 5-10 | Accomplish | Full dashboard reveal | Discovery + Understanding | Engineer the biggest peak moment |
| Day 10-21 | Acclimate | Regular dashboard use | Exploration + Learning | Progressive disclosure, variable insights |
| Day 21-60 | Adopt | Tier 2 journey + habit formation | Growth + Competence | Habit loops, weekly cadence, open loops |
| Day 60-100 | Advocate | Sharing insights, referring others | Pride + Purpose | Make sharing easy, provide language |

**The critical insight:** The Affirm-to-Activate gap (Days 1-7) is where most engagement dies. The entire assessment distribution and completion tracking system exists to bridge this gap. Every design decision during this period should be evaluated against one question: "Does this reduce the admin's doubt that they made the right decision?"

---

## THE 5 Es AS A CONTINUOUS AUDIT

Use Jesse Cole's 5 Es as a continuous audit of the onboarding experience. For every step, ask:

1. **Eliminate Friction:** What's the ONE thing that would make this step easier? Remove it. Then ask again.

2. **Entertain Always:** Does this step have ANY personality? Could someone screenshot this and share it because it made them smile or think? If not, you haven't broken the script enough.

3. **Experiment Constantly:** What's one thing about this step you could A/B test this week? The headline, the button copy, the sequence, the timing? Start with small experiments. The voicemail, the invoices, the parking penguins - those were all small experiments that compounded into a remarkable experience.

4. **Engage Deeply:** Is there a moment in this step where you could do for ONE user what you wish you could do for all of them? A personalized insight, a personal message from Daniel, a detail that shows you were paying attention to THEIR specific situation?

5. **Empower Action:** Does this step end with the user feeling more capable than when they started? Not more informed - more capable. There's a difference. Information is passive. Empowerment is active. Every step should leave the admin or team member feeling like they can DO something they couldn't do before.

## WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE

When this onboarding journey is working, you'll see:

**Quantitative signals:** Org-to-dashboard activation rate above 80% (orgs that create accounts and reach full dashboard within 14 days). Assessment completion rate above 90% per org. Admin return rate within first week above 70%. Time to first team insight under 60 seconds from first dashboard view. Tier 2 journey start rate above 60% of activated admins.

**Qualitative signals:** Admins sharing dashboard screenshots with their teams or peers. Team members asking their admin "Can I see more of my profile?" Admins referencing LWYL insights in team meetings without being prompted. Daniel hearing "I was looking at my dashboard before our call and noticed..." in consulting sessions. The language shifts from "the tool Daniel gave us" to "my team intelligence system."

**The ultimate signal:** When an admin opens the app not because they got a notification, not because Daniel told them to, but because they're about to walk into a meeting and they think "Let me check my LWYL dashboard first." That's the internal trigger. That's the habit. That's when the architecture has won.
